Photo coach: light, background, angles

Buyers decide in the thumbnail. One good set of photos is worth more than any wording in the listing, and a bad photo cannot be rescued by a lower price.

Lighting

  • Shoot in indirect daylight — beside a window, out of direct sun. Daylight keeps the colour of the garment honest.
  • Turn off the overhead lamp. Yellow bulbs and cool white bulbs both tint the item, and the buyer sees the tint when it arrives.
  • Never use flash for the cover shot: it blows out highlights on white fabric and leaves a hard shadow behind the item.
  • Shoot the whole set in one session so every photo has the same colour. Mixed light in one listing looks like two different items.
  • Turn the item, not the camera, if one side is in shadow — rotate the garment a quarter turn and reshoot rather than lifting exposure.

Background

  • Plain and uncluttered: a white wall, a clean sheet, a bare floor. Nothing that competes with the item.
  • Contrast matters — a cream coat on a cream duvet disappears. Pick a background one or two tones away from the item.
  • Clear the frame: laundry piles, cables, pets, mirrors with your reflection and brand tags of other stock all cost trust.
  • Fill the frame. The item should take up most of the image; if it sits in the middle third with empty space, move closer.
  • Iron or steam first. Wrinkles read as "worn out" at thumbnail size and you will retake the whole set otherwise.

Angles — the shot list

  1. Cover: flat-lay straight down, or on a hanger/model if drape is the selling point. Square to the camera, item centred.
  2. Back, laid out the same way as the cover so the set looks deliberate.
  3. Label and size tag close-up — this is what answers "is it real and what size is it really?".
  4. Fabric detail: weave, print, hardware, sole, embroidery. Shot close enough to show texture.
  5. Flaws: holes, pilling, stains, scuffs, colour fade — one photo each, sharp and lit.
  6. Measurements: tape measure across a flat garment (pit-to-pit, length, waist flat). This one photo prevents most returns.

What to retake before you list

  • Blown-out white patches or muddy shadows — move the item, do not fix it with a filter.
  • Colour cast: garment looks yellow, orange or blue compared to real life.
  • Soft focus, camera shake, or the label shot where you cannot read the text.
  • Item smaller than most of the frame, or cropped at a sleeve or hem.
  • Clutter, reflection, creases, or a background that fights the item.
  • Flaw photos that hide the flaw. Show it plainly — buyers who see it up front do not open returns.
  • Stock photos on a used item. Marketplaces remove listings that reuse brand images, so shoot your own copy.
  • Text, watermarks or collages over the photo. Several shops reject or down-rank edited images.

Pricing coach: when to drop, how much, which shop

Price is a number you can change any time — but only after you know which part of the listing is failing. Work through it in this order.

Set the starting price

  1. Search that shop for the same item and sort by sold or recently sold where the shop allows it. Comps you can see sold for are the only benchmark that matters; asking prices are wishes.
  2. Subtract the shop's fee and your postage with the fee take-home calculator so you know what you actually keep.
  3. Add negotiation room. Buyers on every shop here send offers, so list a little above the number you would accept and answer with a counter.
  4. Check the total against shipping: a $40 item with $8 postage you pay is a $32 sale, not a $40 one.

When to drop, and by how much

  • Give a good listing one to two weeks with shares before you touch the price. If it has had no views in a few days, fix the photo instead.
  • Drop in small steps rather than one deep slash — a modest cut keeps you near the comp range and many shops re-surface items with a reduced price.
  • Use each shop's own markdown or offer tool rather than editing silently: those tools push a notification to people who liked the item.
  • Sell season with the weather. Outerwear is searched when it turns cold; as the season closes, price to clear rather than carrying it a year.
  • Never drop below your take-home floor. Run the new price through the fee calculator first — a price cut plus a fee plus postage can put you under zero.
