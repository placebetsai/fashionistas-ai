Shipping: labels, packing, weight and carrier choice

Postage is the one cost in resale that can quietly erase a good price. It is also the one cost most sellers get wrong twice — once by weighing the garment instead of the parcel, and once by discovering the real number after they already agreed a price. Weigh first, know who is paying for the label, and buy it from the cheapest place you are allowed to.

Guide home

The maths: postage and fees come out of the same number

A sale price is not your revenue. Work through it in this order every time you set a price.

  1. Start from the sale price. A $40 item with $8 postage you pay is a $32 sale, not a $40 one.
  2. Subtract the platform's cut. At a 10% fee that is another $4, leaving $28 of a headline $40.
  3. Subtract what the item cost you and any packaging, if you are tracking margin rather than just take-home.
  4. Treat buyer-paid postage as a reimbursement, not income. If the buyer paid $8 for a label that cost you $8, nothing was earned and nothing was lost — but the 10% fee is usually charged on the item plus shipping, so the postage still costs you the percentage on it.
  5. Never agree a discount that puts you under your floor. Run the new number through the fee calculator in the app first: a price cut plus a fee plus postage is how a sale becomes a loss.

Example worked all the way through: $40 item, $8 label you pay, 10% platform fee → $40 − $4 fee − $8 postage = $28 before the item's own cost. If the buyer had paid the $8 themselves, the fee on shipping still costs you $0.80, so you would keep $35.20 − item cost.

Weigh the parcel, not the garment

  1. Pack it first, weigh second. Mailer, box, tape, label — everything. The item's weight and the parcel's weight are different numbers and only the second one gets charged.
  2. Use a kitchen scale. Ones that read to 1 g / 0.1 oz are inexpensive and pay for themselves the first time you avoid a weight-band penalty.
  3. Measure the parcel too. Carriers price bulky-but-light boxes on dimensions as well as weight, so length × width × height matters even when the scale looks friendly.
  4. Round up one band when you are between two. An under-declared weight is charged back to you, or the parcel is delayed at drop-off. A label that covers more than you need costs very little extra.
  5. Write the packed weight down with the listing. If you cross-list the same item to four shops you will otherwise weigh it four times — or guess.

Buying the label: three options

OptionWhen it is the right oneWatch out for
The platform's own labelWhen that platform requires it. Depop-Shipping orders, Vinted's prepaid label and Grailed's eligible US orders all expect their own flow.Shipping a platform-label order with your own label can void your seller protection, or get the order cancelled outright.
A discounted label you buy yourselfWhen you are free to source your own — eBay after the sale, Facebook shipping, or any shop where you have chosen "arrange your own".You must add tracking to the platform's order yourself, and you carry the under-declared-weight risk.
The retail counter priceOnly when you are already at the counter with no other option.It is the most expensive version of the same label. Compare rates before you queue.

Where you are allowed to choose, compare at least two sources on the same weight and same destination before buying. Discount providers exist precisely because retail-counter and platform rates are not the floor.

Packing: the materials and the rules

  • Poly mailer for anything soft: tops, tees, knitwear, jeans. Cheap, light, water-resistant, and light weight is money.
  • Box for shoes, bags, structured jackets and anything that must not be crushed. Shoes in a mailer arrive creased and open a return.
  • Tissue or a bag around the item inside a mailer so it is not loose against the label. Dry clean or wash before packing — a damp parcel arrives musty.
  • Reinforce the seams of anything heavy, and tape in an H pattern on a box so the lid does not pop at the sorter.
  • Cover every old barcode before you attach the new label. A taped-over but still-readable old barcode is the classic cause of a parcel that never scans.
  • Put the label on a flat surface, never across a fold, a strap or the edge of the box.
  • Do not over-box. A shoebox inside a box full of air costs dimensional weight. Fill the gaps instead.

Choosing the carrier

What you are optimisingWhat to weigh up
PriceCompare on the same packed weight to the same destination — not on headline rates. Light and short-distance is where the cheapest service wins.
SpeedMatch the promise in your listing. A slow label on a "ships today" listing is a bad review you wrote yourself.
TrackingNon-negotiable. Untracked parcels are unprotected ones on every shop here, and most platforms will not count them as dispatched.
Insurance and coverCheck what the platform's own protection covers before you pay extra for a policy that duplicates it.
ConvenienceIf the nearest drop point for the cheap carrier is twenty minutes away, the saved amount is not saved.

If a platform issues the label, use it — the protection is worth more than a small saving. Where you may choose, compare on your actual parcel rather than on a rate card.

Hand-off, tracking and deadlines

  1. Ship inside the window the platform shows on the order. Late dispatch is the fastest way to lose a buyer who has already paid, and on several of these shops a missed window cancels the order automatically.
  2. Drop it at a staffed counter when the parcel is valuable, and keep the receipt until delivery is confirmed.
  3. Make sure tracking is attached to the platform's order, not just bought. On the shops where you source your own label you have to enter it yourself.
  4. Check the first scan. A label bought but never scanned is a parcel the platform thinks never left.
  5. Tell the buyer. One message with the tracking number removes most of the anxiety that turns into a dispute.
  6. Save the receipt until the return window has closed — it is your evidence for weight, condition and dispatch date.

Common shipping mistakes

  • Weighing the garment instead of the packed parcel, then paying the difference yourself.
  • Setting the price before working out postage, and discovering the $8 label after agreeing to $40.
  • Shipping a platform-label order with your own label and losing seller protection.
  • Buying the cheapest label and forgetting to enter the tracking number in the order.
  • An old barcode still readable under the new label, so the parcel never scans.
  • Under-boxing: a soft mailer for shoes, no reinforcement on a heavy parcel, gaps left for the item to move.
  • Quoting a delivery speed you cannot hit, then missing it.
  • Not keeping the drop-off receipt, so there is no evidence when a parcel goes missing.
  • Ignoring local pickup where it is available — a local handover costs no label and, on Facebook Marketplace, no selling fee either.
